Long ago, two powerful clans, the Blue Sentinels and
the Green Keepers, protected the Ninefold Seal,
which guarded the ancient knowledge of Pera.
Together, they kept peace across the land. But one
day, a mysterious incident changed everything. One
clan turned against Pera and threatened to destroy
the peace it had once protected. No one knows
which clan betrayed its duty. The truth lies hidden
within a dangerous maze, guarded by fallen stones,
twisting paths, and mysterious coloured tiles.
Now, a robotic guardian has been chosen to uncover
the truth. It must cross the fallen stone barriers,
explore the labyrinth, and collect clues from the blue
and green tiles. The paths and hidden signs hold the
secrets needed to reveal the arrangement of the
ancient Nine-Point Board and discover which clan
has remained loyal to Pera.
In the final chamber, the two clans face their last
battle on the ancient board. Using the clues gathered
from the maze, the robot must uncover the hidden
pattern, identify the winning move, and place the
correct game piece. Only the right move can defeat
the opposing side, restore peace, and unlock the
Ninefold Seal.

2 . 1  A R E N A  L AYO U T  A ND  D I M E N S I O N S
The competition arena measures 2.44 m × 3.66 m and consists of three
connected sections, each measuring 2.44 m × 1.22 m.
The arena contains an obstacle field, a maze with coloured tiles, and a
final 3 × 3 game board.
The main arena specifications are:
Overall arena: 2.44 m × 3.66 m
Individual sections: 2.44 m × 1.22 m each
Black line width: 20 mm
Section 1 obstacles: 10 cm × 10 cm × 10 cm cubes
Section 2 maze: 9 × 4 grid consisting of 36 cells, with a 25 cm × 25 cm
cell pitch measured from wall centreline to wall centreline. 
Maze walls are 12 mm thick and 100 mm high, resulting in a clear
internal area of approximately 23.8 cm × 23.8 cm per cell 
Section 3 game board: 3 × 3 grid, with positions spaced 40 cm
horizontally and 30 cm vertically
Game board outer dimensions: 122 cm wide × 92 cm high, including
the grid lines
Blue game pieces: Cylinders with a 5 cm diameter and 5 cm height
Green game pieces: Cubes measuring 5 cm × 5 cm × 5 cm
Dimensional tolerance: Arena dimensions may vary by up to ±2 mm or
±1% of the specified dimension, as applicable. Teams must ensure that
their robots can operate reliably despite these minor variations.

3 . 2  T R I A L  T WO :  T H E  L A BY R I N T H  O F  H I D D E N  S I G N S
Beyond the fallen stones lies a labyrinth filled with hidden clues. Blue and
green tiles are placed in selected dead-end cells throughout the maze.
However, not every dead-end cell contains a tile. The number of blue and
green tiles will never be equal, ensuring that one colour always has a clear
majority.
Your robot must explore the maze and determine:
The total number of blue tiles.
The total number of green tiles.
The number of right turns along the shortest path through the maze.
The number of left turns along the shortest path through the maze.
Turn Counting: A turn is defined as a 90° change in direction within a cell.
Counting begins when the robot enters the labyrinth and continues until it
reaches the last exit cell. Any turn made inside the last exit cell to align
with or pass through the exit door must not be counted.
The robot must retain all four values because they will be needed to
uncover the arrangement of the game pieces in the final trial.

Discover the Hidden Numbering
The Nine-Point Board follows a special numbering pattern inspired by the
ancient secrets of Pera. The numbers are arranged so that the sum of the
numbers in every row, column, and diagonal is equal.
The numbering begins from the robot's entrance side. Position 1 is
located in the middle cell of the first row, and Position 6 is located on
the robot’s left when viewed from the entrance side, not from the exit
side.
Use this hidden pattern together with the four clues collected in Section 2
to determine the positions of the existing game pieces.
The final trial takes place on the Nine-Point Board, where the Blue
Sentinels and Green Keepers face their final battle.
Identify Your Side
The number of blue and green tiles found in the maze determines the
robot's side:
More blue tiles: The robot represents the Blue Sentinels and uses a
blue cylinder.
More green tiles: The robot represents the Green Keepers and uses a
green cube.
The side with the greater number of tiles represents the Guardian clan
that has remained loyal to Pera.

4 .  P R E PA R I N G  YO U R  R O BOT
Teams must prepare a robot that can complete all three trials safely and
autonomously under the actual conditions of the physical arena. The
robot must be designed not only to perform the required tasks but also to
handle small variations in the arena and its surroundings.
4 . 1  R O BOT  D I M E N S I O N S
The robot must fit within a maximum size of 20 cm × 20 cm × 20 cm.
All components must be securely attached and must not create
hazards or interfere with the arena.
The robot must be able to navigate the obstacle field, explore the
maze, inspect the Nine-Point Board, and place the required game
piece within these dimensional limits.
4 . 2  AU TO N O M O U S  O P E R AT I O N
The robot must complete all trials autonomously, without human
assistance during an attempt.
Remote control is strictly prohibited. Teams must not remotely
control the robot or use external transmitting or receiving equipment
to send commands to or control its movements during an attempt.
The robot must make its own decisions using its onboard controller,
sensors, and programmed logic.
Once an attempt begins, no team member may physically guide,
reposition, or otherwise assist the robot unless instructed by an
official.

The robot must be designed and operated safely throughout the
competition.
It must not damage, scratch, displace, or deliberately interfere with
the arena, its walls, floor markings, obstacles, maze, board, or game
pieces.
Any sharp, loose, unstable, or potentially hazardous components must
be avoided.
The robot must not use any mechanism or action that creates an

Any robot considered unsafe or capable of damaging the arena may be

4 . 3  S A F E T Y  A ND  A R E N A  P R OT E CT I O N
4 . 4  A DA P T I N G  TO  R E A L  A R E N A  C O ND I T I O N S
Teams must ensure that their robots operate reliably under the actual
conditions of the physical competition arena. These conditions may
differ slightly from those encountered during development, simulation, or
practice.
In particular:
Lighting conditions: Sensors must operate reliably under the lighting
present in the competition venue. Teams should avoid relying on a
single fixed lighting condition.
Surface conditions: The robot must maintain reliable traction and
sensor performance on the actual arena surface.
Small dimensional variations: Teams should account for minor
variations in arena dimensions, markings, wall positions, and
component placement.
Section transitions: Small edges or level differences may be present
where adjacent arena sections meet. The boundary between sections
may include an edge of approximately 2 mm or less. The robot must
cross these transitions independently, without lifting, pushing,
repositioning, or other assistance from team members or officials.
Teams are responsible for designing, testing, and calibrating their robots
to handle these conditions reliably.

modifications, including hardware changes, software and code
updates, mechanical adjustments, and sensor calibration.
Teams should use this opportunity to test their robots under different
lighting conditions and ensure reliable operation on the actual
competition surface.
Teams arriving late will only be allowed to use the remaining time of
their allocated session. Lost testing time will not be extended or
provided again through an additional session.

5 . 4  C O M P E T I T I O N  T I M E  A ND  AT T E M P TS
Each team will be given a total competition period of 15 minutes and a
maximum of three official attempts.
Teams should manage their allocated time carefully to achieve the best
possible performance.
There is no separate time limit for each attempt. However, all
attempts must be started within the allocated 15-minute competition
period.
The 15-minute period begins when the judges officially signal the start
of the team's first attempt.
If the 15-minute period expires while an attempt is still in progress,
the team will be allowed to complete that ongoing attempt, provided it
is not otherwise terminated.
The judges will record the actual completion time of that attempt.
Once the 15-minute period has expired, no further attempts will be
permitted, even if the team has not used all three attempts.
The competition period will not be extended because of failed or
terminated attempts.

board, or other equipment.

immediately.
